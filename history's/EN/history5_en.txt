A girl was walking in the park with her grandmother and saw a shop with big, beautiful dolls behind the glass.

She wanted one so badly that she begged:

— Let’s go in and buy a doll!

— What shop? — her grandmother asked.

— Right there! — the girl pointed. But her grandmother saw nothing and thought the girl might be sick, so she took her home to check her temperature.

But the girl was healthy. The next day, when she went out again, the store was back — now standing right in their yard.

She ran home.

— Mom, there’s a doll store! And the dolls are so big and beautiful! Please buy me one!

But when the mother came with her — the store was gone.

That night, the girl woke up. Her room was bright, like daytime. The light was coming from the window — no, not a window anymore, but a glass door into the doll shop.

The girl grabbed her piggy bank and went in before the store vanished again.

— You want to buy a doll? — asked the shopkeeper.

She looked at him and was frightened — he looked like a giant spider. His arms were like long claws.

But then he took a doll from the shelf and placed it beside her.

It was so beautiful and big — almost as tall as she was. It looked almost alive.

The girl thought she didn’t have enough money in her piggy bank.

— How much is this one? — she asked.

The man pointed at the piggy bank. She gave it to him.

— Can I take the doll now?

He nodded.

The girl grabbed the doll and rushed toward the door, afraid he’d change his mind.

But once she was back in her room, the doll opened its eyes, grabbed her by the throat, and began to strangle her.

She was so scared she fainted.

When she woke up, she was standing on a shelf in the doll shop — a doll herself. She couldn’t move, couldn’t scream or cry.

All she could do was wait — for someone to buy her.

And then she would strangle them… and live their life.

Until the spider-like shopkeeper returned her to the shelf.
