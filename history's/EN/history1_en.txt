In one city, there was an old tower.

People said that if you looked into the tower’s only window at sunset, you could see another city — one made entirely of gold.

They also said that you could enter that golden city through the window. You had to grab something golden and get out quickly, before the city’s people caught you. Because if they caught you, they would bury you alive in a golden coffin.

One evening, a boy was walking past the tower and saw a bright light in the window. He came closer, looked in — and saw the golden city. The walls and roofs of the houses were pure gold. Right near the window, a girl was sitting and playing with gold bars like they were regular stones. No one else was in sight.

The boy quickly climbed through the window, jumped down, grabbed two bars — and turned to go back. But the window no longer showed his city.

He looked around. The girl was standing and watching him.

— You can’t go back, — she said. — No one ever has.

The boy jumped to the ground.

— Are they going to bury me in a golden coffin? — he asked.

The girl nodded.

— Why do you do that? You want the gold? I’ll give it back! — he said, handing over the bars.

— No, it’s not about the gold, — the girl said. — You came from a dead city. And you’re dead too. So we have to bury you. The dead don’t live among the living.

— But I’m alive! — the boy shouted.

— No, you’re dead, — she shook her head. — Look at your hands.

The boy looked — and saw the hands of a corpse.

He became frightened and started to cry.

People began coming out of the golden buildings. Two of them carried a golden coffin.

— It’s a pity you’re dead, — said the girl. — We could’ve played together.

— But I’m alive! — the boy screamed. — I just need to go back! Please let me go!

But the people from the golden city didn’t listen. They grabbed the boy, laid him in the coffin, and nailed the lid shut with golden nails.
