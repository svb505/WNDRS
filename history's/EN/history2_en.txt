One evening, a girl was walking alone by the river.

She usually walked there with her mom or dad. But this time she went by herself, even though her mother had told her not to leave the yard.

“Nothing will happen to me,” the girl thought. “I know this place.”

But in the evening, everything looked different. There were no people around — only the sounds of children playing somewhere nearby.

The girl followed the sounds and soon saw kids running through the sand and water, tossing something back and forth.

They were having so much fun, she ran toward them.

They stopped when she got close but didn’t chase her away. Instead, they waved at her, inviting her to join.

The girl tried to catch whatever they were throwing, but every time she got close, a boy would snatch it before her. He was too fast.

She started getting angry, but he always outran her. And something about his face seemed familiar — like she’d seen it before, but couldn’t remember where.

When the sun set, the children suddenly stopped playing. One by one, they walked into the river, dove in — and didn’t come back up.

The girl was scared. Especially when someone grabbed her hand and started pulling her toward the water.

She screamed and tried to break free — but the grip was too strong.

Then she saw the face of whoever was holding her. It was blue and swollen, with black eyes, and it smelled like mud and rot.

She realized she’d been playing with drowned children.

She screamed again but couldn’t escape. The dead boy’s hand was like a clamp.

Just then, the same fast boy ran up. In his hands, he held a dead head — that’s what the children had been throwing. The head was alive, baring its black teeth and trying to bite his fingers.

The boy handed it to the drowned one, who grabbed it and let go of the girl.

She immediately ran home.

Only later did she realize: that boy had saved her twice — first from the head, then from the dead child.

At home, she finally remembered where she’d seen his face — in an old photo. It was her mom’s younger brother, who drowned in the river long ago when he was a boy.
